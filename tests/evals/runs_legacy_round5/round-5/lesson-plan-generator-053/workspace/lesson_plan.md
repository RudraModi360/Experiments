# Lesson Plan: Adding and Subtracting Fractions with Unlike Denominators

**Grade Level:** 5  
**Duration:** 45 minutes  
**Topic:** Adding and Subtracting Fractions with Unlike Denominators (Common Denominators / Least Common Multiple)  
**Subject:** Mathematics  

---

## 1. Objectives (SWBAT)
By the end of this 45-minute lesson, Students Will Be Able To:
1. **Find** the least common denominator (LCD) for two fractions with unlike denominators.
2. **Convert** fractions with unlike denominators into equivalent fractions with like denominators.
3. **Add and subtract** fractions with unlike denominators accurately in both equation and word problem formats.

---

## 2. Materials & Preparation
* **For Students:**
  * Whiteboard and dry-erase marker (or math notebook/pencil)
  * Fraction strips or pattern blocks (optional tactile manipulatives)
  * Exit Ticket (printed slips, 1 per student)
* **For Teacher:**
  * Document camera or whiteboard/markers
  * Anchor chart: "Steps for Adding/Subtracting Fractions with Unlike Denominators"
  * Practice worksheets / task cards for guided/independent practice

---

## 3. Lesson Structure & Timing (45 Minutes)

### **Part A: Warm-Up & Hook (5 minutes)**
* **Activity:** Equivalent Fractions Quick Check & Mental Math.
* **Prompt:** Write $\frac{1}{2}$ and $\frac{3}{4}$ on the board. Ask students: *"Can we add these directly right now? Why or why not?"*
* **Discussion:** Elicit from students that the denominators (sizes of the pieces) are different. Review how to find equivalent fractions (e.g., $\frac{1}{2} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{3}{6}$).

### **Part B: Direct Instruction / Modeling (10 minutes)**
* **Concept Introduction:** Explain that to add or subtract fractions, the pieces must be the exact same size—we need a **Common Denominator**.
* **Step-by-Step Method (LCM approach):**
  1. **Find the Least Common Multiple (LCM)** of the denominators to use as the Least Common Denominator (LCD).
  2. **Convert** each fraction into an equivalent fraction with the new denominator by multiplying numerator and denominator by the same factor.
  3. **Add or Subtract** the numerators while keeping the denominator the same.
  4. **Simplify / Reduce** the result if possible (and convert improper fractions to mixed numbers if applicable).
* **Worked Example 1 (Addition):** $\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4}$
  * Denominators: $3$ and $4$. Multiples of $3$: $3, 6, 9, \mathbf{12}, 15...$ Multiples of $4$: $4, 8, \mathbf{12}, 16...$ LCD = $12$.
  * Convert: $\frac{1 \times 4}{3 \times 4} = \frac{4}{12}$ and $\frac{1 \times 3}{4 \times 3} = \frac{3}{12}$.
  * Add: $\frac{4}{12} + \frac{3}{12} = \frac{7}{12}$.
* **Worked Example 2 (Subtraction):** $\frac{3}{4} - \frac{1}{2}$
  * LCD = $4$.
  * Convert: $\frac{3}{4}$ stays $\frac{3}{4}$; $\frac{1}{2} = \frac{2}{4}$.
  * Subtract: $\frac{3}{4} - \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{4}$.

### **Part C: Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
* **Activity:** "Think-Pair-Share" & Whiteboard Challenge.
* **Task:** Put two problems on the board for students to work through with a partner on mini-whiteboards:
  1. $\frac{2}{5} + \frac{1}{3}$ (Answer: $\frac{11}{15}$)
  2. $\frac{5}{6} - \frac{1}{4}$ (Answer: $\frac{7}{12}$)
* **Teacher Role:** Circulate the room, listen to partner discussions, clear up misconceptions about finding common denominators or multiplying numerators incorrectly.

### **Part D: Independent Practice (12 minutes)**
* **Activity:** Tiered Independent Practice Worksheet / Station work.
* **Problems include:**
  * Standard numerical addition and subtraction of unlike fractions.
  * One real-world word problem (e.g., *Maria used $\frac{2}{3}$ cup of flour for pancakes and $\frac{1}{4}$ cup of flour for muffins. How much total flour did she use?*).
* **Differentiation / Support:** 
  * *Support:* Provide multiplication charts / fraction bars for students needing visual aid with LCM and equivalent fractions.
  * *Extension:* Challenge students with fractions involving mixed numbers or three fractions (e.g., $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{6}$).

### **Part E: Assessment & Closure (8 minutes)**
* **Closure Discussion:** Briefly review the key steps. Ask a student volunteer to explain *why* we find a common denominator before adding or subtracting.
* **Exit Ticket:** Administer a quick 2-question exit ticket to be completed independently before leaving class:
  1. Evaluate: $\frac{3}{8} + \frac{1}{4} =$ _______
  2. Evaluate: $\frac{7}{10} - \frac{2}{5} =$ _______

---

## 4. Assessment Plan
* **Formative Assessment:** Teacher observation during guided practice and circulation during independent practice; whiteboard check responses.
* **Summative / Diagnostic Assessment:** The 2-question **Exit Ticket** scored out of 2 points to measure individual mastery of finding LCD and computing sums/differences of fractions with unlike denominators.
